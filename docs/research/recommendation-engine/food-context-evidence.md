# Food/context prediction: evidence and a falsifiable MealRec experiment

Research checked 2026-10-17. Recommendation: learn from MealRec choices and personal food history first; treat weather as an inexpensive feature hypothesis. The papers below establish different outcomes. A food mention, recipe rating, grocery purchase, add-on sale, logged consumption and satisfying meal choice are not interchangeable labels.

## Empirical evidence actually inspected

Numbers below belong to their original experiments, not expected MealRec improvements. Cross-paper values use different preprocessing and candidate sets and cannot rank the methods against one another. Each linked paper's methods/results were inspected; the last column includes our interpretation.

**Evidence boundary:** these are reasons to run an experiment, not proof that weather improves a person's next-dish ranking. I found no convincing isolated weather-specific personalization result among the inspected papers. The strongest contrary reading is that history predicts habitual replenishment and easy repeat foods; MealRec users may seek variety specifically when habits fail. A model can score well offline while adding little decision value.

Weather need not *cause* preference to predict it. But seasonal menus, price/promotions, stock, holidays, store/location, customer mix, delivery access and weather-dependent attendance can produce associations that fail elsewhere. A rainy-day increase in delivery demand can coexist with unchanged dish preference. Hold those alternatives/exposures constant when testing dish ranking.

## Proposed minimal protocol — a recommendation, not a published result

**Define the target before features.** Primary target: which eligible meal a person confirms choosing from a displayed slate, including an explicit “none acceptable.” Separately record actual consumption, subsequent liking/satisfaction and purchase confirmation when available. Never relabel merchant spending as a dish or a click as eating. Missing diaries, absent feedback and unshown meals are unknown, not dislikes; choosing one shown meal supplies a relative comparison only within that slate.

MealRec first needs timestamped events containing user/session, candidate and rendered meal IDs, order, exclusions, availability/price if applicable, model/version, shown context and the final outcome. Preserve historical events instead of overwriting the latest feedback. Distinguish a confirmed rejection from abandonment/censoring. Log exposure probability for any deliberate randomization. Hard dietary exclusions stay outside learned scores.

**Ablation ladder:** score the same eligible items with the same outcome labels, splits and comparable tuning budgets. Keep the current heuristic as a frozen challenger, not a probability baseline.

1. **Population baseline:** item popularity, with feasible availability, price/offers and calendar controls where relevant. Check against the current heuristic.
2. **Stable personal taste:** add prior explicit food/ingredient/cuisine likes and dislikes, pooling sparse histories toward population estimates. Separately test personal frequency and recency; retain the simpler variant if equivalent.
3. **Occasion/context:** add meal occasion, weekday/time and coarse location/setting available at prediction time. Test item/context interactions with shrinkage, not a separate model for every person/context.
4. **Weather:** add temperature, precipitation and deviation from local seasonal norms, plus a small prespecified set of food/weather interactions. Compare against calendar/location controls; test forecast-based weather separately from climatology.
5. **External purchase history:** only after obtaining consented, timestamped item-level records with defensible mapping to meals/ingredients. Compare with stage 4 on **the identical covered-user cohort**, and test with/without MealRec history. Report household versus person attribution and mapping errors.

For a promising combined model, also remove each block from the full model. Incremental value depends on other blocks and interactions; ladder order alone cannot attribute unique value. Compare weather against a richer seasonal baseline and shuffled-within-location/season weather as a diagnostic, not as the production model.

**Holdouts and leakage checks:**

- Use common chronological cutoffs: earlier training, later tuning, untouched final future test; repeat rolling-origin development checks. Keep whole sessions/slates together. Warm-user tests can legitimately use those users' earlier histories, never later outcomes.
- Run a separate completely held-out-user test for cold start. Report zero-history and short-history performance; adaptation may use only a designated historical prefix. Do not remove unseen users/items silently.
- Reserve geographic areas and later seasons when data permit, and report same-area future versus unseen-area results separately. No evidence of geographic transfer if all data come from one place.
- Fit encoders, normalizers, item popularity, neighbors, feature selection and climatologies on training data only. Use transaction **availability** time, refunds/revisions and macro-data release dates where relevant.
- Reconstruct weather available at the actual serving timestamp and forecast horizon: archive forecast issue time or record the served payload. Tomorrow's observed weather, centered rolling windows, daily end-of-day summaries and retrospective reanalysis are not tomorrow's available forecast. Include missing/stale-weather cases and serving latency.
- Freeze the final test before source/model selection; do not repeatedly choose features on it. Randomize a small eligible exposure subset if safe, or use a randomized online comparison before claiming recommendation benefit. Off-policy estimates require logged propensities and support; weighting cannot recover never-exposed alternatives.

**Measures and decision rule:**

- Primary predictive metric: user-balanced held-out choice log loss including “none”; Brier score and reliability plots assess probability calibration. Raw heuristic scores are not calibrated probabilities. Check calibration by occasion, history depth and weather regime.
- Ranking: top-1 choice accuracy, Recall@5/MRR or nDCG for the recorded target **within its observed displayed choice set**. These labels do not judge unshown catalog items. Full-catalog evaluation requires a separate labeling/exposure design with recorded assignment probabilities and support for the stated estimand; otherwise omit it. For actual multi-item purchases, use basket Recall/nDCG and separate repeat from new items. Do not compare metrics from different candidate sets.
- Product outcome: confirmed acceptable choice, decision time and later satisfaction/consumption; retain rejection/constraint-violation guardrails. Click/add-on conversion alone does not settle whether users wanted a better meal.
- Compare paired predictions on identical events. Estimate uncertainty by resampling users and time blocks; shared weather shocks require date/location-aware uncertainty, not independent-row confidence intervals. Report coverage and subgroup failures, not only a pooled average.
- Before testing, set a smallest useful gain relative to integration, privacy, latency and maintenance costs. Estimate needed data from observed variance, repeat-user clustering and that decision margin; there is no universal magic sample size.

**Discard/defer a source** if serving-time availability or consent fails, labels/items cannot be aligned, coverage is too selected, missingness breaks predictions, or improvement disappears after temporal/location/season controls and matched-cohort comparisons. Keep it only when uncertainty supports useful incremental gain on untouched future data, cold/short-history fallbacks remain sound, and an online check confirms user benefit. “Not enough evidence yet” is distinct from “proved useless.”

## Inspection record and remaining uncertainty

Actually read: methods/results of all seven linked papers. Publicly accessible copies were also downloaded and text extracted locally when the web fetcher failed. Gerg details use the accessible author preprint associated with the [published article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretconser.2025.104649); the publisher's final text was not retrievable. No datasets were collected, joined or used to train a model. The 2026 household weather-shocks SSRN abstract and older store-sales abstracts were screened but are not supporting evidence here.

Unknown: MealRec traffic, repeat-use rates, attainable labels, weather variation, consented purchase coverage and dish mappings. Next evidence needed: a small prospective event log with confirmed choices and enough repeated decisions to compare personal frequency, occasion and weather on identical future events. None of the published numbers supplies that answer for MealRec.
