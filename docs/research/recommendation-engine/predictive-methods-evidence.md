# Predicting a meal someone wants: methods evidence

Research date: 2026-10-17. This note recommends experiments, not an implementation or a claim that MealRec has training data. Baseline facts are supplied by the shared brief; live traffic and label counts remain unknown.

**Answer.** Yes: learned models can combine a person's past responses, meal attributes, and current circumstances to predict a defined response. The defensible claim is “probability of choosing this meal among these alternatives, under these conditions.” Purchasing, consuming, liking afterwards, and wanting now are different outcomes. Combining more columns does not create missing outcome labels or identify an unconstrained inner preference.

**Define the target before choosing the model.** Recommended first target: an immediate, explicit choice from the actually displayed, eligible meals, including “none of these.” This is stated current preference; validate it against later confirmed choice and satisfaction separately. Record the decision time, available/eligible pool, rendered alternatives and positions, presentation policy, and outcome. This estimates choice within the offered set: rendering does not prove attention or reveal all real-world alternatives. Unselected displayed alternatives are alternatives lost in this decision, not dislikes; unshown meals supply no negative labels. An unanswered prompt is missing, unless the explicitly defined target is “no in-app selection within a stated interval.” That behavioral outside option must not be relabeled “wants no food.” Hard dietary exclusions and known unavailability remove alternatives before scoring.

A useful first specification is:

`score(u,i,t) = meal_bias(i) + (population_taste + user_deviation(u)) · meal_features(i) + context(t) × meal_features(i) + purchase_friction(u,i,t)`

`P(choice=i) = exp(score_i) / [exp(score_none) + Σ_available_displayed_j exp(score_j)]`.

This is our proposed adaptation of repeated-choice logit. Revelt and Train's original application used repeated appliance choices, allowing person-specific coefficients and varying alternative sets; it was not food preference prediction. Their survey-based estimates also needed calibration against actual purchases. [Revelt & Train 1998, §§II–III and V.B](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~train/papers/revelttrain.pdf)

Here, stable taste uses cuisine/ingredient/texture/spice attributes and only history available before the decision. Shrink user deviations toward the population when history is scarce. Current context can include meal time, supplied hunger/occasion, and weather available at serving time. **A shared weather main effect cancels between meals in this softmax:** weather must interact with meal attributes, or change meals' utility relative to the outside option. “Cold × hot soup” is a testable interaction, not an assumed positive coefficient. Actual quoted price, discounts, preparation/delivery burden and budget can enter purchase friction when known; unavailable prices stay missing. Exposure/position also affects observed choice: model or standardize presentation instead of interpreting position as taste. These are specification recommendations, not identified causal effects.

| Candidate | How combined data enter; useful role | Evidence and reason to stop |
|---|---|---|
| Regularized logistic / conditional logit; hierarchical or mixed logit if justified | Explicit meal × context interactions; partially pooled user taste coefficients. Binary logistic fits explicitly elicited yes/no responses; grouped conditional choice fits one chosen alternative plus outside option. | Most inspectable first learned challenger. Mixed logit accounts for repeated-person heterogeneity but adds distribution/estimation assumptions; ordinary logit restricts substitution. Appliance evidence establishes the method, not meal accuracy. [Revelt & Train](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~train/papers/revelttrain.pdf) |
| Shallow boosted trees / CatBoost | Same tabular inputs, nonlinear thresholds and interactions, for example temperature × soup × recent soup history. Use a choice-compatible grouped objective or explicit binary-response labels; independent card probabilities do not automatically sum to a choice distribution. | CatBoost addresses leakage from categorical target statistics and boosting; its paper evaluates general classification benchmarks. It does not repair future-history leakage, unlogged exposure or ambiguous labels. Promote only if it beats the simpler model. [Prokhorenkova et al. 2018, §§3–6](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/file/14491b756b3a51daac41c24863285549-Paper.pdf) |
| Context-aware factorization with meal metadata | Shared low-dimensional interactions among user, meal, ingredients and context can pool sparse combinations; metadata supplies information for meals without item history. | Requires repeated, overlapping observations to learn useful collaborative factors. A new user still needs population/elicited preferences. Factorization is not a substitute for exposure labels. [Rendle et al. 2011, §4](https://www.ismll.uni-hildesheim.de/pub/pdfs/Rendle_et_al2011-Context_Aware.pdf) |
| Separate preference and purchase heads; share only what helps | Separate labels/heads for explicitly elicited current desire, purchase conditional on exposure/feasibility/price, and later satisfaction. Train only heads with observed labels, and compare sharing against separate models. | MMoE studied task relatedness and engagement versus satisfaction in large-scale Google content recommendation. This motivates separating outcomes, not installing that architecture in MealRec. Negative transfer remains possible. [Ma et al. 2018](https://research.google/pubs/modeling-task-relationships-in-multi-task-learning-with-multi-gate-mixture-of-experts/) |

First test last-meal/recency/repetition covariates and explicitly supplied occasion. Add sequence complexity only if ordering adds held-out signal beyond them; current overwritten feedback cannot establish that. This is our recommendation. |

There is some directly relevant food evidence: Rendle et al.'s Food experiment used 6,360 ratings from 212 people on 20 menu items, with context describing real versus imagined hunger and hunger level. Context-aware factorization and Multiverse models outperformed context-free factorization there. However, the outcome was a rating, the protocol used fivefold cross-validation, and there was no demonstrated weather, payment, or prospective MealRec effect. It is feasibility evidence with substantial limits. [Rendle et al. 2011, §§5.1–5.2.3, Fig. 6](https://www.ismll.uni-hildesheim.de/pub/pdfs/Rendle_et_al2011-Context_Aware.pdf)

**One hypothetical training row, not existing MealRec data.** A decision has `{mushroom soup, salad, rice bowl, none}`; one row represents soup:

| Field block | Example available before response |
|---|---|
| Identity/content | `decision=d104; user=u17; meal=soup7; hot=1; mushroom=1; spice=0` |
| Stable/history | `prior mushroom likes=3/4 explicit responses; last soup=2 days ago`, calculated before this session |
| Current context | `19:00; hunger=high; rain=yes; temperature=14°C`, observed/forecast information timestamped before recommendation |
| Feasibility/friction/exposure | `eligible=yes; price_quote=9000 KRW; discount=0; position=2; rendered=yes; logging_policy=R1`, with slate/order probability retained by that policy |
| Separate labels | `chosen_now=1; purchased=unknown; consumed=unknown; satisfaction=unknown` |

The other displayed meals and outside row get `chosen_now=0` for the grouped choice loss, not `disliked=1`. A general historical “like” must not silently become a context-specific current-choice label. In MealRec's present generic dish catalog, a merchant purchase requires a verified dish/offer mapping before it can label this row.

**Observational purchases need interpretation, not just ingestion.** A purchase may reflect price, stock, habit, other household members, a promotion or what was shown; nonpurchase can mean no exposure. Schnabel et al. demonstrate selection bias in recommender training/evaluation and derive propensity-weighted estimators assuming nonzero observation probabilities; unknown propensities require estimation. Their empirical setting includes rating data, not an identification result for grocery desire. For MealRec, log recommendation exposure and any controlled exploration probabilities prospectively. Propensity weighting cannot recover outcomes for alternatives never supported by the logging policy, or turn an unidentified merchant total into a meal. Causal identification of weather is unnecessary for prediction under the same process; prediction can still fail when presentation, supply or population changes. [Schnabel et al. 2016, §§3, 5–6](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/schnabel16.pdf)

**Grocery-to-dish transfer and external priors.** Recommendation: a purchased SKU mapped confidently to an ingredient can inform a stock/feasibility feature or a weak long-run taste prior. It does not label a particular prepared dish, cooking method, eater, date of consumption or satisfaction. Ingredient overlap alone misses preparation and substitutions. Learn and validate such transfer only with aligned grocery histories and subsequent dish outcomes; otherwise keep it a declared hypothesis.

Recipe1M+ learns representations from recipe text and food images and tests image–recipe retrieval; its described records contain recipes/images and some course/nutrition annotations, not MealRec people’s current wants. This supports a potential content representation prior, not pretrained personal desire. Existing MealRec metadata is the simpler starting representation. External preference data, if obtainable and permitted, might supply pooled parameters after item/outcome alignment and local validation; it cannot supply matching user identities by assumption. [Marín et al. 2019, §§II.D, III–VI](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.06553v2)

**Progression without pretending there is a universal sample threshold.**

1. **No aligned decision labels:** keep the heuristic as a benchmark; use hard constraints, explicit onboarding responses and content features. Population priors must be labeled as priors. Establish append-only decision/exposure/outcome records; weather and receipts alone are features without supervision.
2. **Sparse labeled occasions:** fit a regularized pooled model, then shrunk user taste effects where supported. Start with a small, specified context-interaction block. Use learning curves and uncertainty to decide whether personalization is supported.
3. **More repeated users and variation:** compare boosted trees and context-aware factorization on identical prediction targets/choice sets. Add purchase features only for verified, temporally prior records; add a purchase head only when its target is actually observed. Additional heads and sequences require incremental evidence, not a traffic milestone.

For MealRec this changes the next question from “which smarter formula?” to “which observable outcome improves beyond the current heuristic?” The current latest-per-user–meal feedback and absent impressions, confirmed choices and occasion histories do not support the proposed choice experiment retrospectively. No claim here establishes that external purchase access exists.

**Strongest counterargument and a baseline-winning experiment.** Sparse, occasional users and a small catalog may make stable content taste plus simple rules sufficient; hunger, availability and social constraints may be mostly unobserved. More flexible interactions could learn exposure artifacts and noise.

Pre-register a prospective comparison: current heuristic, popularity/content-only baseline, regularized taste-only model, the same model plus context interactions, then a tree challenger with the same feature blocks and comparable tuning budget. Maintain identical eligible alternatives, labels and serving-time features. Include weather, prior verified purchases and price/promotion in separate ablations; do not change all together. Use an earlier training period, subsequent validation, and untouched final future period. Evaluate returning users using only their earlier history; separately test cold-start users withheld as whole groups. Do not split repeated observations randomly across time.

Compare choice-set log loss including “none,” ranking, calibration, and user/day-clustered uncertainty. The heuristic's internal sampling probabilities are not calibrated human choice probabilities: compare its ranking directly, or fit its score-to-probability mapping and outside-option intercept on validation data before comparing log loss. A randomized final policy comparison should measure confirmed meal choice and follow-up satisfaction, with response rates reported separately. Prefer the baseline if incremental benefit is practically negligible/uncertain, calibration deteriorates, or user benefit fails to improve. Feature importance or fitting historical purchases better cannot override that result.

Unresolved: local label volume, user recurrence, achievable outcome follow-up, dish-level purchase matching, context coverage, and whether grocery or external representations transfer. No model was fitted and no sample-size or uplift claim was inferred from these papers.
